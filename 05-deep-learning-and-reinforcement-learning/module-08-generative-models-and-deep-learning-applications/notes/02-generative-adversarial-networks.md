# Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN)

## 1. Introduction to GAN

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are generative models that use two neural networks to learn how to generate realistic data.

The two networks have different roles:

- Generator
- Discriminator

They are trained in a competitive process.

## 2. Main Components of GAN

### Generator

The generator creates artificial data samples.

It receives a random noise vector as input and transforms it into generated data, such as an image.

### Discriminator

The discriminator evaluates an input sample and predicts whether it comes from real data or was generated by the generator.

Its task is to distinguish real samples from generated samples.

## 3. How GAN Works

The training process involves the following steps:

1. The generator receives a random noise vector.
2. The generator produces a generated sample.
3. The discriminator receives real samples and generated samples.
4. The discriminator predicts whether each sample is real or fake.
5. The discriminator is trained to distinguish between real and generated data.
6. The generator is trained to produce samples that can better fool the discriminator.
7. The process is repeated to improve both networks.

## 4. GAN Architecture

```text
Random Noise
     |
     v
 Generator
     |
     v
Generated Sample
     |
     v
 Discriminator <----- Real Data
     |
     v
Prediction:
Real or Fake
```

The generator tries to produce realistic samples, while the discriminator tries to identify generated samples.

## 5. GAN Training Process

The course material describes the following general training approach:

- Initialize the network weights randomly.
- Generate an image from a noise vector.
- Let the discriminator predict whether the image is real or fake.
- Train the discriminator.
- Train the generator.
- Repeat the process to improve the generated results.

## 6. Generator vs. Discriminator

| Component | Main Role |
|---|---|
| Generator | Creates artificial samples from random noise |
| Discriminator | Distinguishes real samples from generated samples |

Both networks work together through an adversarial training process.

## 7. Key Takeaways

- GAN consists of a generator and a discriminator.
- The generator creates artificial samples from random noise.
- The discriminator predicts whether samples are real or fake.
- Both networks are trained through a competitive process.
- The goal is to improve the generator's ability to produce realistic samples.
